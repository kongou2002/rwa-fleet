Rental cars, owned in shares

Own part of a rental car. Collect your part of the rent.

Each car is split into shares at a fixed price. When it sells out, the rental operator buys the car and puts it to work. Every rent payment is split on-chain: 90% to shareholders, 10% to the manager.

How the money moves

Four steps. The contract runs each one, not a person.

  1. 1

    A car is listed

    The manager lists a car as a fixed number of shares at a fixed USDC price, with a funding deadline.

  2. 2

    Investors buy shares

    Your USDC waits inside the contract. If the car does not sell out by the deadline, you take all of it back.

  3. 3

    Sold out: operator is paid

    The last share sold sends the full amount to the rental operator, who buys the car and rents it out.

  4. 4

    Rent comes back

    The operator pays rent in per car. 90% is shared by holders in proportion to their shares, 10% goes to the manager.

Safeguards, stated plainly

These rules are in the contract code. Nobody can change them after deployment.

  • The manager never holds investor money

    The manager lists cars and earns a fee on rent. It has no function that can move your purchase money.

  • The operator is paid only at sell-out

    Purchase money stays in the contract until the last share is sold. Then it goes to the operator in the same transaction.

  • Missed deadline means a full refund

    If a car is not fully funded in time, anyone can close the sale and every buyer takes back exactly what they paid.

  • The 10% fee is fixed by the contract

    Every rent payment is split 90 / 10 at the moment it arrives. The manager cannot raise its fee.

Limits

This is a testnet demo. The USDC here is a test token with no value.

The chain cannot see the real car or its real income. It records who reported what, and it enforces how the money is split. Whether the operator buys the car and reports rent honestly depends on the operator and on the legal agreement behind the documents hash.
